About Innermind

Technology, designed around the human being.

Innermind Technologies is an affective-computing research initiative developing foundational software architectures capable of perceiving and respecting changing human cognitive and emotional states.

MISSION

Solve Human Context Friction

We are investigating how intelligent interfaces can adapt fluidly to reduce cognitive overload and support mental clarity during demanding tasks.

VISION

Architect Deep Cognition

Our vision is a future where computing operates as an empathic, context-aware collaborator rather than a rigid, static utility.

APPROACH

Research-Led & Principled

We synthesize cognitive psychology, machine inference, and ethical systems design to establish robust foundations for human-centric computing.

We are not trying to make software louder or faster.
We are engineering software to be profoundly more aware. — Innermind Manifesto
Research Philosophy

An interdisciplinary convergence.

Inferring human state responsibly requires combining affective computing, cognitive psychology, and low-latency inference systems. We treat this intersection as a rigorous research inquiry — distinguishing clearly between foundational concepts, active engineering prototypes, and long-term scientific explorations.

Long-Term Ambition

Foundational infrastructure, not a single app.

Our long-term ambition is for the Emotional Operating System to serve as an infrastructure layer. This framework will empower developers, health researchers, and AI builders to integrate state-aware capabilities into assistive systems across multiple industries.

Ethical Foundation

Principles we hold ourselves accountable to.

01

Human Agency First

State inference must empower user autonomy, avoiding coercive feedback loops or manipulation.

02

Zero Extractive Monetization

Emotional telemetry must never be brokered for behavioral advertising or unconsented surveillance.

03

Scientific Candor

We describe our capabilities with precision, avoiding hyperbole and respecting empirical boundaries.
