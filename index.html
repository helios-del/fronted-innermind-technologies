The problem

Most software has no idea how you actually are.

Interfaces are built to register what you click, type or buy — rarely how focused, fatigued, stressed or receptive you are in that moment. Every interaction is treated as context-free, as if the same person in a different state should be met with an identical experience.

Computing should not only understand what you do.
It should increasingly understand how you are. — Innermind Technologies
The platform

The Emotional Operating System.

A proposed architecture that takes in human signals, infers a multidimensional state, and adapts the experience in response — conceived as a foundational layer other systems could build on.

01 · Input

Human signals & context

Interaction patterns, timing and contextual information begin to form an evolving picture of the person behind the session.

02 · Inference

Multidimensional state

Signals are interpreted across up to fifteen psychological dimensions rather than collapsed into one static profile.

03 · Adaptation

Personalised response

The inferred state is designed to inform how an experience adjusts — its pacing, tone or content — as the person's state evolves.

04 · Output

A more responsive experience

The goal is technology that meets people where they are, instead of treating every visit as identical.
